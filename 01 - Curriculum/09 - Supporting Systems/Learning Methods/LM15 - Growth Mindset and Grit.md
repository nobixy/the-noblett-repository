# LM15 — Growth Mindset and Grit
*Deep dive + project ([[DR-009 - Learning Method Deep Dives|DR-009]]). Hub: [[LM00 - Learning Methods Hub|Learning Methods]] · Method in practice: [[how-i-study]] §1*

> [!NOTE] Evidence: **Contested; small effects**

## In plain words
**Growth mindset:** the belief that ability grows with work and good strategies.

**Grit:** sticking with a long-term goal.

Both are good attitudes to hold. Neither is a study technique.

## The evidence, honestly
Sisk et al. (2018), two meta-analyses: the link between mindset and grades is weak (r ≈ 0.10), and mindset interventions have very small effects overall. They help somewhat more for low-income or struggling students.
Credé et al. (2017): grit overlaps heavily with conscientiousness and is only moderately related to performance. Its "perseverance of effort" part is the useful bit.
Honest takeaway: don't expect mindset alone to make you better. Use it to keep going while the techniques that do work ([[LM02 - Retrieval Practice|retrieval]], [[LM09 - Spacing and Spaced Repetition|spacing]]) do the heavy lifting.

## Using it in EECS
- Read a failing test or a red autograder as information, not a verdict.
- Compare yourself with your own log from months ago (Appendix E, failure mode 8), not with others.

## Common mistakes
- Praising effort that uses bad strategies.
- Grinding the same failing approach and calling it grit.

## 🔨 Project: Then-vs-Now
A Python script that finds your daily note from 30 days ago and today's, and prints their "what I did" and "stuck on" sections side by side.
- **Done when:** you ran it once a month for 2 months, and each time wrote one line about what got easier.
- **Time:** about 2 h, counted inside [[P1 - Learning How to Learn|P1]]'s existing hours (replaces the optional *Ultralearning* skim and the bash-template part of Build 2; the SM-2 app absorbs the Week 9 Leitner app (4 h)).
