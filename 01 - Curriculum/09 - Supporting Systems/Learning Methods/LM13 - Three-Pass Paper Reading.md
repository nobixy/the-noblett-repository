# LM13 — Three-Pass Paper Reading
*Deep dive + project ([[DR-009 - Learning Method Deep Dives|DR-009]]). Hub: [[LM00 - Learning Methods Hub|Learning Methods]] · Method in practice: [[how-i-study]] §1*

> [!NOTE] Evidence: **Practitioner advice; not tested**

## In plain words
Read a paper in three passes:
1. A 5–10 minute skim: title, abstract, headings, conclusions.
2. A careful read that skips the proofs.
3. A deep pass where you could re-create the work yourself.

Stop after any pass if the paper isn't worth more.

## The evidence, honestly
This comes from Keshav (2007), "How to Read a Paper": three pages of advice from an experienced researcher, not a study.
It is the standard in CS graduate programs, and it pairs well with retrieval: after each pass, write what you remember.

## Using it in EECS
- Use it on systems papers (UNIX, MapReduce, Raft) and on datasheets: pass 1 is the features table, pass 2 is the electrical characteristics, pass 3 is the register map you'll actually program.

## Common mistakes
- Starting with pass 3 on page one.
- Never writing the pass-1 summary (Keshav's "five Cs").
