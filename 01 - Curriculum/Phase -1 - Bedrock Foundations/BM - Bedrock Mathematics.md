# BM — Bedrock Mathematics: Arithmetic from First Principles

> [!QUOTE]
> "Arithmetic is not the art of calculation; it is the art of understanding what numbers are and what we can do with them."  
> — **Paul Lockhart, *Arithmetic***

Most people believe they are "bad at math" because they were taught arithmetic as a collection of rote recipes and magic incantations (e.g. "borrow the one", "invert and multiply", "negative times negative is positive"). 

Here, you start from absolute zero—not as a child memorizing flashcards, but as a philosopher examining **why** numbers behave as they do.

## 🧱 The 6 Foundational Pillars

### 1. Counting, Grouping & Place Value
- What is counting? One-to-one correspondence between physical objects and symbols.
- Why base-10? Why not base-2 (binary) or base-12 (duodecimal)?
- **The Genius of Positional Notation:** The number $342$ does not mean "3, 4, 2". It means $3 \times 10^2 + 4 \times 10^1 + 2 \times 10^0$.
- The historical breakthrough of **Zero**: zero as nothing, and zero as a place-holding column shifter.

### 2. The Nature of the Four Operations
- **Addition:** Combining distinct sets or moving rightward along a continuous dimension.
- **Subtraction:** Difference, distance between two points, or reverse movement.
- **Multiplication as Geometry:** Multiplication is *not* just repeated addition; it is the creation of a new dimension. $3 \times 4$ is the area of a rectangle 3 units by 4 units. This geometric intuition makes the distributive law $a(b + c) = ab + ac$ visually obvious.
- **Division:** Two distinct meanings:
  1. *Partitive (sharing):* Dividing 12 apples equally among 3 friends (how much does each get?).
  2. *Quotative (measuring):* How many 3-foot segments fit into a 12-foot board?

### 3. Fractions, Ratios & Proportions
- A fraction $\frac{a}{b}$ is not two separate numbers stacked on top of each other; it is a single unified quantity representing a relationship or a division.
- **Why do we need common denominators to add?** You cannot add 2 apples and 3 quarters directly because they lack common units. The common denominator creates a common unit of measurement.
- **Why does "invert and multiply" work for division?** $\frac{a}{b} \div \frac{c}{d}$ asks: *how many $\frac{c}{d}$'s fit into $\frac{a}{b}$?* Explain this using quotative division on a physical ruler without using the formula.

### 4. Negative Numbers & The Symmetry of Zero
- Negative numbers as opposites or directions (debt vs. credit, left vs. right).
- **The Ultimate Test:** Why is $(-1) \times (-1) = +1$?
  - *Algebraic proof via the Distributive Property:*
    $$(-1) \times (1 + (-1)) = (-1) \times 0 = 0$$
    $$(-1) \times 1 + (-1) \times (-1) = 0$$
    $$-1 + (-1) \times (-1) = 0 \implies (-1) \times (-1) = +1$$
  - *Intuitive / geometric proof:* Multiplying by $-1$ represents a $180^\circ$ rotation on the number line. Rotating by $180^\circ$ twice returns you to the positive axis ($360^\circ$).

### 5. Primes: The Atoms of Mathematics
- Factors, divisors, multiples.
- **The Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic:** Every integer $> 1$ is either prime or can be uniquely factored into a product of primes (up to the order of factors).
- Primes are the chemical elements of arithmetic; composite numbers are molecules.

### 6. The Bridge to Algebra (Variables as Generalized Arithmetic)
- An equation is a balanced physical scale.
- A variable ($x$) is an unknown weight on the scale.
- The Golden Rule of Algebra: You may perform any operation (add, subtract, multiply, divide) as long as you perform it equally to both sides to maintain the balance.
