# BW — Bedrock English: Grammar & Sentence Architecture

> [!QUOTE]
> "Clear thinking becomes clear writing; one can't exist without the other."  
> — **William Zinsser, *On Writing Well***

Writing is not an art of mystical inspiration; it is **software engineering for human attention**. A sentence is a logical circuit: it takes characters, connects them through verbs of action, and delivers meaning to a reader's short-term working memory without cognitive overflow.

## 🧱 The 5 Pillars of Sentence Architecture

### 1. The Anatomy of the Clause
Every thought in the English language resolves into a **clause** with two mandatory components:
- **The Subject:** Who or what the sentence is about (the protagonist).
- **The Predicate:** What the subject does, experiences, or is (the action/state).

**The Core Sentence Patterns:**
1. *Subject + Intransitive Verb:* "The processor halted."
2. *Subject + Transitive Verb + Direct Object:* "The compiler emits assembly."
3. *Subject + Linking Verb + Subject Complement:* "The pointer is null."
4. *Subject + Verb + Indirect Object + Direct Object:* "The kernel sent the process a signal."

### 2. Phrases vs. Clauses & Hierarchy
- **Phrase:** A group of words acting as a single part of speech without a subject-verb pair (*"in the cache"*, *"running at 3 GHz"*, *"having finished the loop"*).
- **Independent Clause:** A clause that can stand alone as a complete sentence (*"The test passed."*).
- **Dependent / Subordinate Clause:** A clause that cannot stand alone, marked by a subordinating conjunction (*"Because the memory was freed"*, *"Although the socket closed"*).
- **The Subordination Principle:** Put your main idea in the independent clause; put background context, causes, or conditions in subordinate clauses.

### 3. Punctuation as Logic Gates
Punctuation marks are traffic control signals for human working memory:
- **Period (`.`):** Complete halt. The working memory buffer is dumped and reset for the next proposition.
- **Comma (`,`):** Brief pause. Separates introductory elements, items in a series, or non-essential parenthetical information. Never separate a subject from its verb with a single comma.
- **Semicolon (`;`):** The equal-balance junction. Connects two independent clauses that are so closely linked in logic that a period would separate them too violently.
- **Colon (`:`):** The announcement / delivery mark. The first clause sets up an expectation; what follows the colon delivers the goods (an explanation, a list, or an expansion).
- **Em-Dash (`—`):** An abrupt structural break or emotional aside.
- **Parentheses (`()`):** A whispered confidential detail that the reader could skip without losing the core thread.

### 4. The Williams Clarity Engine (Eliminating Mud)
The two master rules that instantly transform weak, muddy sentences into surgical prose:
- **Rule 1 (Characters as Subjects):** Make the real actors of your story the grammatical subjects of your sentences.
- **Rule 2 (Actions as Verbs):** Locate the real action of the sentence in the main verb. Eliminate **nominalizations** (verbs strangled into heavy abstract nouns).
  - *Bad / Nominalized:* "An examination of the data was conducted by the team, leading to the occurrence of a system crash." (Actors and actions are buried in "examination", "conducted", "occurrence").
  - *Good / Active:* "The team examined the data, and the system crashed."

### 5. Cohesion & The "Given-Before-New" Flow
- Sentences in a paragraph must interlock like cognitive puzzle pieces.
- Start each sentence with information that is **already familiar** or "given" from the previous sentence.
- Place the **new, surprising, or complex** information at the very end of the sentence (the stress position).
