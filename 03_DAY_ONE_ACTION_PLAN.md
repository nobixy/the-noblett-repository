#### Crucial Field Protocol: Windows 10/11 BitLocker & Device Encryption Pre-Flight
> **CRITICAL LIABILITY & DATA ACCESS SHIELD:**  
> Virtually all modern consumer OEM laptops and desktops (Dell, HP, Lenovo, Acer) running Windows 10/11 Home or Pro enable automatic **Device Encryption / BitLocker** the moment the user signs in with a Microsoft Account.  
> 
> **Two Fatal Traps to Avoid:**
> 1. *Drive Inaccessibility:* Offline live environments (Ubuntu Live or Hiren's BootCD PE) cannot mount or read BitLocker-encrypted NTFS volumes without the client's 48-digit numerical recovery key.
> 2. *Permanent System Lockout:* Changing UEFI boot priority or booting untrusted external USB media trips the motherboard's TPM Platform Configuration Register (PCR) measurements. On subsequent reboot back into Windows, the system will halt at the blue BitLocker Recovery screen demanding the 48-digit key. If the customer does not have this key, they are permanently locked out of their operating system and personal data.
> 
> **Mandatory Pre-Flight Procedure Before Booting Ventoy/WinPE:**
> - If Windows boots, check encryption status immediately: run `manage-bde -status` in an elevated command prompt or navigate to `Settings > Privacy & Security > Device Encryption`.
> - If encryption is active or pending, have the customer log into `https://account.microsoft.com/devices/recoverykey` (or shortcut `aka.ms/myrecoverykey`) on their smartphone or secondary device.
> - Verify that their 48-digit BitLocker recovery key is visible and matches the Key ID displayed on screen.
> - Write down the 48-digit recovery key on the Intake Sheet and have the customer initial it **BEFORE** inserting diagnostic USB media or entering UEFI firmware settings.
> - If the client machine is unbootable and the customer cannot locate their recovery key, assist them in retrieving it from their Microsoft account before attempting offline disk recovery or cloning.
