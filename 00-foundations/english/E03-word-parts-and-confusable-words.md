# E03 — Word Parts and Confusable Words

**In this stage you will:** learn to take long words apart into pieces (prefix, root, suffix) so you can spell and understand words you have never seen; master the 40 word pairs people mix up most; learn the core technical vocabulary; and re-measure your spelling against your E01 baseline.

**Time:** about 20 hours over 3 weeks.

**Before you start:** E02 done. You know your top two error tags.

---

## Why word parts matter

Long words are usually short words snapped together, like functions built from smaller functions. *Un + necessary = unnecessary*. Once you see the parts, two things happen:

1. **Spelling gets easier.** The parts keep their spelling even when the sound changes. *Mis + spell = misspell* (two s's, one from each part). *Dis + appear = disappear* (one s, because *appear* has none). The double letters that trip people up are usually just two parts meeting.
2. **Vocabulary explodes.** Know that *trans* means "across" and *mit* means "send," and you understand *transmit, transmission, transmitter*, and can guess *emit* and *remit*. Technical English is built almost entirely from Latin and Greek parts. Learning 50 parts unlocks thousands of words.

**Connection to code:** naming. A function that converts a value is `convert`; a thing that converts is a `converter`; the act is `conversion`; something that can be converted is `convertible`. Good names come from knowing how English builds words. You will name hundreds of things in every project.

## Part 1 — Prefixes, roots, and suffixes

A **prefix** goes at the start and changes the meaning. A **root** carries the core meaning. A **suffix** goes at the end and often changes the word's job (noun, verb, adjective; see E04).

### The joining rule

**When you add a prefix, never add or drop letters. Just join.**

| Prefix + word | Result | Why it looks hard |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| un + necessary | u**nn**ecessary | one n from *un*, one from *necessary* |
| mis + spell | mi**ss**pell | one s from each |
| dis + appear | di**s**appear | *appear* has no s |
| dis + satisfied | di**ss**atisfied | one s from each |
| im + mediate | i**mm**ediate | one m from each |
| re + commend | re**c**o**mm**end | *re* + *commend* (one c, two m's, as in *command*) |
| inter + rupt | inte**rr**upt | one r from each |
| il + legal | i**ll**egal | one l from each |
| over + run | ove**rr**un | one r from each |

> **Spelling hook for -cede / -ceed / -sede:** only three words end in **-ceed**: *proceed, succeed, exceed*. Only one ends in **-sede**: *supersede*. Everything else is **-cede**: *precede, concede, recede*. Note that *procedure* drops an e from *proceed*. Put this on one flashcard.

### Suffixes that change a word's job

| Suffix | Makes a… | Examples |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| -tion, -sion, -ment, -ness, -ity, -ance, -ence | noun (a thing or idea) | connection, version, statement, readiness, security, performance, reference |
| -er, -or | noun (a person or thing that does it) | compiler, server, router, computer · processor, editor, monitor, operator |
| -ize, -ify, -ate | verb (to make or do) | optimize, normalize, verify, simplify, activate, calculate |
| -able, -ible, -al, -ive, -ous, -ful, -less | adjective (describes) | readable, visible, digital, active, dangerous, useful, wireless |
| -ly | adverb (describes how) | quickly, safely, recursively |

**-er or -or?** No perfect rule. *-or* is more common on Latin-based words (processor, operator, editor, vector, sector, monitor, iterator, allocator, constructor). *-er* is more common on plain English words (computer, server, reader, writer, parser, buffer, router, compiler). When unsure, look it up and add it to your log.

## Part 2 — Confusable words

These are real words, so spell checkers will not catch them. That makes them the most dangerous errors in professional writing. Learn them in groups of five per day.

| Words | Meaning and example | Hook |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **their / there / they're** | *their* = belongs to them (their code) · *there* = a place, or "there is" (put it there; there is a bug) · *they're* = they are | **there** has **here** inside it (a place) · the apostrophe in **they're** replaces the *a* of *are* |
| **your / you're** | *your* = belongs to you (your file) · *you're* = you are | test: can you say "you are"? Then *you're* |
| **its / it's** | *its* = belongs to it (the function returns its value) · *it's* = it is / it has | test: can you say "it is"? Then *it's*. *His, hers, its*: no apostrophes in any of them |
| **to / too / two** | *to* = direction, or before a verb (go to; to run) · *too* = also, or excessive (too big) · *two* = 2 | *too* has too many o's |
| **then / than** | *then* = time, next (then restart) · *than* = comparison (faster than) | th**e**n = tim**e**; th**a**n = comp**a**re |
| **affect / effect** | *affect* = verb, to change something (heat affects speed) · *effect* = noun, the result (the effect was huge) | **A**ffect = **A**ction (verb); **E**ffect = **E**nd result (noun) |
| **accept / except** | *accept* = take, agree to (accept the input) · *except* = not including (all except one) | **ex**cept = **ex**clude |
| **lose / loose** | *lose* = fail to keep (lose data) · *loose* = not tight (a loose wire) | *loose* has a loose extra o |
| **whether / weather** | *whether* = if (check whether it exists) · *weather* = rain, sun | **whether** has **he**; weather has **eat** (sun and rain) |
| **were / where / we're** | *were* = past of *are* (they were fast) · *where* = place (where is it?) · *we're* = we are | **where** has **here** |
| **who's / whose** | *who's* = who is / who has · *whose* = belongs to whom (whose laptop?) | apostrophe = missing letters |
| **quite / quiet** | *quite* = very, fairly · *quiet* = not loud | qui**et** ends like *diet*, two syllables |
| **then / them** | different words; a common typo | read aloud while proofreading |
| **lead / led** | *lead* (present, rhymes with *need*) · *led* (past) · *lead* the metal (rhymes with *bed*) | *LED* lights *led* the way |
| **passed / past** | *passed* = verb (the test passed) · *past* = earlier time, or beyond (in the past; past the limit) | if you can say "has passed", use *passed* |
| **choose / chose** | *choose* = now (rhymes with *news*) · *chose* = past (rhymes with *rose*) | |
| **breath / breathe** | *breath* = noun · *breathe* = verb (the e is silent and makes the vowel long, E02 Pattern 2) | |
| **principal / principle** | *principle* = a rule or belief · *principal* = main, or a head person | princip**le** = ru**le** |
| **complement / compliment** | *complement* = completes (two's complement!) · *compliment* = praise | compl**e**ment comple**te**s |
| **discrete / discreet** | *discrete* = separate, countable (discrete math) · *discreet* = careful, quiet | in disc**ret**e, the t **separates** the e's |
| **advice / advise** | *advice* = noun · *advise* = verb | ad**vice** like *ice* (a thing) |
| **ensure / insure** | *ensure* = make sure · *insure* = buy insurance | |
| **brake / break** | *brake* = stop a car · *break* = smash, or a pause (`break` in a loop) | |
| **peace / piece** | *peace* = calm · *piece* = a part | a **pie**ce of **pie** |
| **know / no / now** | *know* = understand · *no* · *now* = at this time | **k**now → **k**nowledge |
| **new / knew** | *new* = not old · *knew* = past of *know* | |
| **hear / here** | *hear* = with your ear · *here* = this place | **ear** in **hear** |
| **write / right / rite** | *write* = make text · *right* = correct, or direction · *rite* = ceremony | **w**rite: **w**ords |
| **by / buy / bye** | *by* = near, or "done by" · *buy* = pay for · *bye* = goodbye | **u** **buy** |
| **though / through / thorough / thought / tough / threw** | *though* = however · *through* = in one side, out the other · *thorough* = complete, careful · *thought* = past of think · *tough* = hard · *threw* = past of throw | say them in spelling voice; write all six in one sentence once a week |
| **fewer / less** | *fewer* = countable things (fewer bugs) · *less* = amounts (less memory) | can you count them? → *fewer* |
| **i.e. / e.g.** | *i.e.* = "that is" (restates exactly) · *e.g.* = "for example" | **e.g.** = **e**xample **g**iven |
| **a lot / alot** | always **two** words: *a lot* | you can't write "alittle" either |
| **all right / alright** | **all right** is the safe choice in formal writing | |
| **every day / everyday** | *every day* = each day (I study every day) · *everyday* = ordinary (an everyday task) | |
| **maybe / may be** | *maybe* = perhaps · *may be* = might be (it may be broken) | |

### Tech-specific: one word or two?

Many tech terms are **one word as a noun or adjective**, but **two words as a verb**. This is an extremely common error in technical writing, and getting it right makes your writing look professional.

| Noun / adjective (one word) | Verb (two words) |
| :-- | :-- |
| the **login** page; your **login** | **log in** to the server |
| the **setup** guide | **set up** the machine |
| a **backup** | **back up** your files |
| the **shutdown** | **shut down** the computer |
| a **lookup** table | **look up** the value |
| a **checkout** | **check out** the branch |
| a **printout** | **print out** the report |
| the **startup** time | **start up** the service |
| a **cleanup** step | **clean up** the files |
| a **workaround** | **work around** the bug |

**Test:** if you can put *-ed* or *-ing* on it, it is the verb: "I *logged in*", "I am *setting up*". Verbs are two words.

## Part 4 — The three-week routine

| Week | Lesson focus (15–20 min/day) | Spelling (10–15 min/day) |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1 | Prefixes and the joining rule (Mon–Tue) · roots (Wed–Thu) · suffixes (Fri) | your error log + Group 1 |
| 2 | Confusables, five pairs a day (Mon–Fri) | your error log + confusables in sentences |
| 3 | Tech one-word/two-word (Mon) · Practice Sets (Tue–Wed) · Self-check and retest (Thu–Fri) | your error log |

**Daily practice for word parts:** pick 5 words from anything you read today (an error message, a man page, a news story). Split each into parts. Write the meaning of each part. Look up the ones you are unsure about on etymonline.com. This is a why-ladder [W] on vocabulary.

**Daily practice for confusables:** write one sentence of your own for each word in today's set, about your real life. *Write them by hand, without a checker.* Next day, before the new set, rewrite yesterday's sentences from memory [R].

**Copywork [C]:** move to **Level 2** in week 2 if you met the Level 1 standard (fewer than 3 spelling errors, three times in a row). Try the first paragraph of the Python Tutorial, section 1 ("Whetting Your Appetite"). Notice how many words you can now split into parts.

### Practice Set 3 — Take it apart (8 words)

Split each word into parts and give the meaning of each part, then the meaning of the whole word.

1. transmit · 2. interrupt · 3. hexadecimal · 4. subroutine · 5. inspect · 6. construct · 7. binary · 8. autonomous

<details>
<summary>Answers (Set 3)</summary>

1. **trans** (across) + **mit** (send) = to send across (a signal, a message)
2. **inter** (between) + **rupt** (break) = to break in between (stopping the processor's current work to handle something)
3. **hexa** (six) + **deci** (ten) + **-al** = relating to sixteen (base-16 numbers)
4. **sub** (under, smaller) + **routine** (a regular procedure) = a smaller procedure inside a bigger one
5. **in** (into) + **spect** (look) = to look into
6. **con** (together) + **struct** (build) = to build together
7. **bi** (two) + **-ary** (relating to) = having two parts or values (0 and 1)
8. **auto** (self) + **nom** (law, rule) + **-ous** = ruling itself; acting on its own
</details>

## Self-check and retest (end of week 3)

This is the end of the spelling-focused part of the track. Retest everything from E01 and compare.

1. **Repeat E01 Test A** (the same 60-word dictation). Record score.
2. **Repeat E01 Test B** (15-minute free-write, error rate). Record score. **Target: under 1 error per 100 words.**
3. **Confusables quiz:** make 20 sentences with blanks yourself, using 20 different pairs from Part 2. Wait two days. Fill them in cold. Target: **19/20**.
4. **Feynman [F]:** in half a page, explain to a friend why *misspell* has two s's and *disappear* has one. Then explain why spell checkers miss *their/there* errors.
5. **Write the comparison:** in `english/baseline-YYYY-MM-DD.md` (today's date), put your E01 numbers next to today's. Write three sentences about what changed.
