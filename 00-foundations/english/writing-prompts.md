# Writing Prompts

**Over 150 prompts for daily writing that's actually fun.** They're grouped by English stage, so each one practises the skill you're learning right now. There are also "any stage" prompts, constraint games, prompts tied to your projects, and speaking prompts.

---

## How to use this

**The daily fun write (10–15 minutes, at least 4 days a week).** This is your "500 words" habit, made easier: quantity first, fun first.

1. Pick a prompt from **your stage** or from **Any stage**. (Roll a die or use `shuf -n 1` on a list if choosing is hard.)
2. **Write fast** for 10 minutes. No stopping, no spell checker, no deleting. Messy is fine.
3. **Then 3 minutes of checking** — only for *this stage's skill*. In E02, check suffix spellings. In E06, check commas. In E08, check that each paragraph has one point.
4. Put every misspelling you find into your spelling log.
5. Once a week, pick your favourite piece and **revise it** properly (E08 checklist from E08 on). Keep the best ones in `~/workbench/english/fun-writes/` — in six months you'll see a clear change.

**Log it:** in your daily journal, mark `words_500: true` on days you do it, and note the word count.

**Sizes by stage:** E01–E03: 3–8 sentences · E04–E06: 8–15 sentences · E07–E08: 1–3 paragraphs · E09–E10: up to a page. Bigger is fine, but never required.

## E01–E03: Words and spelling

1. **Word smuggler.** Write a short story (5–8 sentences) that uses five words from your spelling log. Underline them. Check them last.
2. **The confusion.** A detective story in 6 sentences where *their, there,* and *they're* are all clues.
3. **Shopping list poem.** A list of 10 things you'd buy for a computer lab, each with one adjective. Check every spelling against a dictionary afterwards.
4. **Acrostic.** Write your first name down the page. Start each line with that letter, using a technical word (Module 01 vocabulary).
5. **The word that won't stick.** Write a letter to the word you misspell most. Complain about it. Then forgive it and spell it correctly 5 times at the end.
6. **Alliteration attack.** Six sentences, each with at least four words starting with the same letter. ("Seven silent servers sent signals…")
7. **Weather vs whether.** A short weather report that uses *whether* three times correctly.
8. **Word builder.** Pick one root from E03 (*port, struct, rupt, spect…*). Write a mini-story using five words built from it.
9. **Prefix story.** Write about a day where everything went *un-, re-, mis-, dis-*: "I misread my alarm, re-set it, and…" (8 sentences).
10. **Hidden words.** Find a word inside each of five long words (*separ-AT-e, be-LIE-ve*) and write a sentence that uses both.
11. **Compound creatures.** Invent five creatures by joining words (*backupbear, loginlizard*). One sentence describing each. Check the noun/verb rule from E03 (backup vs back up).
12. **The spelling bee announcer.** Write the commentary for a dramatic spelling bee final. The winning word is one from your log.
13. **Ten-word headlines.** Write five news headlines about your week, each exactly ten words.
14. **Sound alike.** One sentence each for *peace/piece, brake/break, hear/here, write/right, by/buy*. Make each sentence funny.
15. **Dictionary dive.** Open a dictionary (or etymonline.com) at random. Pick a word you don't know. Write three sentences: what it means, where it comes from, and one sentence using it.
16. **Robot vocabulary.** A robot learns five new English words today. Write its diary entry.
17. **The 100-word challenge.** Write exactly 100 words using only words from E01's Core List A (plus names). Hard and fun.
18. **Rule poem.** Turn one spelling rule from E02 into a four-line rhyme you'll remember. ("Hop gets two, hope gets one…")

## E04: Parts of speech

19. **Noun-only poem.** Describe your desk using only nouns (and *and*). Then rewrite it adding one adjective to each noun.
20. **Verb-only action scene.** A chase scene written almost entirely in verbs. ("Running. Jumping. Slipping. Grabbing…") Then rewrite it as full sentences.
21. **Build your own Mad Lib.** Write a 6-sentence story, then replace ten words with blanks labelled by part of speech (NOUN, PAST-TENSE VERB…). Fill it in a week later without looking at the original.
22. **Name the code.** Rename ten objects in your room as if they were variables and functions: `kettle.boil_water()`, `is_door_locked`. Explain each choice in one sentence.
23. **Adverb overload.** Write a paragraph with far too many adverbs ("he very quickly and extremely loudly…"). Then cut every adverb you can. Count how many you removed.
24. **Preposition map.** Give directions from your bed to your front door using at least ten different prepositions.
25. **Pronoun mystery.** Write a short scene where pronouns make it unclear who did what. Then fix every unclear pronoun.
26. **Interjection opera.** A conversation between two people using mostly interjections (*Oh! Hmm. Wow. Oops.*) plus a few nouns. Make the story still understandable.
27. **The adjective auction.** Sell a boring object (a paperclip) using as many exciting adjectives as you can. Then write the honest version with three.
28. **Part-of-speech switch.** Use each of these words as a noun and a verb in one funny sentence: *test, file, run, back up, update*.

## E05: Sentences and verbs

29. **Commit log of your life.** Write today as 10 git commit messages, imperative and capitalised. ("Fix breakfast: replace burnt toast", "Add 20 minutes of math")
30. **Incident report.** In past tense, report what happened when your kettle (or phone, or bike) "failed" this week. Who did what?
31. **The life of a packet.** In present tense, describe a message travelling from your phone to a friend's phone: subject–verb–object, simple sentences.
32. **Five patterns, five sentences.** One sentence for each of the five patterns (E05 Part 2) about a robot that's learning to cook.
33. **Tense time machine.** Write the same event three times: past ("I fixed it"), present continuous ("I am fixing it"), and future ("I will fix it").
34. **Active or passive detective.** Write a short report full of hidden actors ("Mistakes were made…"). Then rewrite it naming every actor.
35. **Irregular verb story.** Use ten irregular past tenses from E05 (*broke, brought, taught, wrote…*) in one story.
36. **Instructions for a toddler robot.** Seven imperative sentences to teach a robot to make a sandwich. One action per sentence.
37. **The agreement trap.** Write sentences with tricky subjects ("The list of errors…", "Each of the cables…"). Get every verb right, then check with E05 Part 4.
38. **Diary of a CPU.** One day in the life of a processor, past tense. ("At 8:00, the user pressed a key. I woke up…")
39. **Sports commentary.** Narrate yourself doing a chore as live sports commentary, present continuous. ("And our champion is reaching for the sponge… the crowd goes wild…")

## E06: Punctuation

40. **CPU and RAM argue.** A dialogue with quotation marks: the CPU is impatient; the RAM is forgetful.
41. **Semicolon diary.** A diary entry where every pair of closely related sentences is joined with a semicolon. (Then judge: did it help?)
42. **Colon menu.** A restaurant menu for a café run by programmers. Use colons to introduce each list.
43. **Apostrophe crime scene.** Write a sign with five wrong apostrophes ("Fresh egg's!"). Then write the corrected sign and explain each fix.
44. **List, list, list.** A packing list for a trip to Mars in full sentences, using serial commas.
45. **The terminal transcript.** Describe a terminal session in prose, putting every command in `code format`. ("I typed `ls -la` and saw…")
46. **Comma changes everything.** Write five pairs of sentences where one comma changes the meaning ("Let's eat, Grandma").
47. **Dash and parentheses.** An excited text message to a friend about something you built — using an em dash once and parentheses once, correctly.
48. **Numbers in writing.** A short report on your week with ten numbers in it, following E06's number rules (units, digits, words).
49. **Hyphen hunt.** Describe your laptop using five hyphenated compound adjectives (*16-inch, well-worn, battery-powered…*).

## E07: Joining ideas

50. **Excuses a printer gives.** Ten excuses, each with *because*, *although*, or *unless*.
51. **Robot butler rules.** Ten if–then rules for a robot butler. ("If the milk is empty, …")
52. **Cause or coincidence?** Write five sentences that say "after" and five that say "because" about the same events. Which claims are actually true?
53. **Sentence combining game.** Take 12 short sentences from your journal. Combine them into four good ones.
54. **The great debate.** "Cats are better programmers than dogs." Two paragraphs: one with *but/however*, one with *although/whereas*.
55. **Parallel lists.** Write your morning routine as one sentence with a parallel list of at least five actions.
56. **Run-on rescue.** Write the longest run-on sentence you can (one breath, no full stops). Then fix it four different ways.
57. **Unless story.** A story where everything depends on one condition: "Unless the light turns green, …"
58. **Code comments that explain why.** Write five `# because …` comments for five lines of code you wrote recently.
59. **Two timelines.** Write what happened "while" you were doing something else. ("While the download ran, …")

## E08: Paragraphs and clarity

60. **Explain your favourite game** to someone who has never played it. Three paragraphs, each with a clear topic sentence.
61. **Why I'm learning this.** One paragraph, one idea. Then revise it three times, making it shorter each time without losing anything.
62. **Old before new chain.** A six-sentence story where each sentence starts with something from the end of the previous one.
63. **The fog machine.** Write the foggiest paragraph you can (nominalisations, passive voice, "it is important to note that…"). Then rewrite it clearly. Count the words saved.
64. **One-paragraph movie review** of a film you love: point first, then three reasons.
65. **The skim test.** Write a five-paragraph piece about your neighbourhood. Then read only the first sentences: do they tell the whole story?
66. **Explain a sound.** One clear paragraph describing a sound to someone who can't hear it (a dial-up modem, a coffee machine, rain on a tin roof).
67. **Before and after.** Take a paragraph you wrote in your first month (from your journal). Revise it with the E08 checklist. Put both side by side.
68. **Teach a trick.** Explain one math trick from your math stages (×9 with ×10, the digit-sum test) in one clear paragraph.
69. **Postcard from a byte.** A short postcard from a byte travelling through your computer. Clear enough for a child to follow.

## E09: Technical description and instructions

70. **Instructions for an alien:** how to make a cup of tea. Numbered steps, conditions first, expected results. Then test it on a friend who must follow it *literally*.
71. **Bug report for your cat** (or a sibling, or the weather). Title, environment, steps to reproduce, expected, actual.
72. **README for your fridge.** What it is, quick start, usage, known issues.
73. **Rewrite bad error messages.** Collect three unhelpful messages you've seen ("Error 0x80070057", "Something went wrong"). Rewrite each to say what happened and what to do.
74. **How it works: a zipper** (or a bicycle bell, a toilet cistern, a lock). Purpose → parts → flow → edge case, with a sketch.
75. **Release notes for your week.** "Version 2026.43: Added…, Fixed…, Known issues…"
76. **The definition game.** Define ten words using category + difference ("A router is a device that…"). Then define three everyday things the same way (a chair, a sandwich, a friend).
77. **Recipe as a program.** Write a recipe as pseudocode with variables and loops.
78. **Troubleshooting table** for a common problem at home (Wi-Fi not working, toast burning). Problem → likely cause → what to do.
79. **Assembly instructions for nonsense furniture.** Invent a flat-pack furniture item and write its instructions — clear, even though the furniture is absurd.
80. **Explain your terminal prompt.** Describe every part of your shell prompt and what it tells you.
81. **The FAQ.** Write five frequently asked questions (and clear answers) about one of your projects.

## E10: Argument and design

82. **Tabs vs spaces.** Argue for the side you *don't* believe in. Claim, reasons, evidence, the other side, response.
83. **Design doc for a time machine.** Goals, non-goals, alternatives (with a trade-off table), risks.
84. **Trade-off table: pizza toppings.** At least four criteria, honest cells, a decision, and what would change your mind.
85. **Persuade future you** to keep studying on bad days. Use evidence from your own logs.
86. **The best tool.** Argue that one tool (your editor, a multimeter, a pencil) is the most important tool you own.
87. **Proposal to your city:** one small improvement (a bench, a crosswalk button). Problem, proposal, cost, alternatives.
88. **The steelman.** Find an opinion you disagree with. State it in its strongest form, better than its supporters do. Then respond.
89. **Lab report on a kitchen experiment.** Does water boil faster with a lid? Question, prediction, setup, results, conclusion.
90. **Review a design.** Write a polite, specific review of an app you use: what works, what confused you (reader-experience language), must-fix vs nice-to-have.
91. **Write the "alternatives considered" section** for a decision you made in real life (where you live, what you eat for breakfast).
92. **The rejected feature.** Argue *against* adding a feature to one of your projects. Why is leaving it out the better design?

## Constraint games (great once a week)

Constraints make writing a puzzle, and puzzles are fun.

| Game | Rule |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Lipogram** | Write 100 words without the letter **e**. (Trains vocabulary.) |
| **One-syllable** | A paragraph using only one-syllable words. (Trains plain style.) |
| **Exactly 50** | A complete story in exactly 50 words. |
| **No "very," "really," "just," "basically"** | A page without these four words. |
| **Every sentence ≤ 10 words** | Explain something technical. |
| **No adjectives** | Make it vivid with nouns and verbs only. |
| **Start each sentence with the next letter** | A, then B, then C… for 26 sentences. |
| **Reverse** | Tell a story from the ending back to the beginning. |
| **Second person** | Write a scene using *you* as the main character. |
| **Copycat** | Write a new paragraph in the style of your current copywork author, on a different topic. (The best follow-up to [C].) |

## Prompts tied to your projects

**Module 01**
- **Nib's diary:** the tiny computer describes its first program from the inside ("Someone put 02 48 into my memory…").
- **Relay's lost message:** a message dropped by the gremlin tells its story.
- **Burrow Jr.'s family tree:** the shell describes its children and what happened to each.
- **Pagelet's first website:** write a page for your site in PML about why you started this curriculum.

**Modules 02–04**
- **A song for Tone Loom:** write the lyrics, then compose the melody in your song format.
- **A Worldfile world:** every room description is a writing exercise — write the rooms first, as prose.
- **The Crosswalk's complaint:** a traffic light writes a letter to the pedestrians who keep pressing the button.
- **The heater's story:** the thermostat's resistor describes its day of overshoots.

**Modules 05–13**
- **The B+tree's family reunion:** nodes split and grow; describe it as a family history.
- **The packet's passport:** a Courier packet describes its journey through loss and retransmission.
- **The kernel's first breath:** Seedling describes the moment after OpenSBI hands it control.
- **The browser's first page:** Glimpse describes reading its first HTML document.

## Speaking prompts (for your weekly recording [F])

Talk for 60–120 seconds without notes, then listen back and count fillers.

1. Explain your current stage's main rule to a 12-year-old.
2. What did you build this week? Show it while you talk.
3. Describe the most stubborn bug you've had, as a story.
4. Teach one math trick with a pen and paper on camera.
5. Explain how something in your kitchen works.
6. Argue for or against: "Everyone should learn to program."
7. Explain why you're doing this curriculum, as if to a job interviewer.
8. Give a 60-second "elevator pitch" for one of your projects.
9. Explain a word's history (from etymonline).
10. Describe your study routine and one thing you'd change.
11. Review a video from [courses-and-videos.md](../../courses-and-videos.md): what was clear, what wasn't?
12. Retell a short news story in plain English, in under a minute.
