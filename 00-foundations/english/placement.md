# English Placement Diagnostic

**Find out where to start the English track.** Every math stage opens with a cold diagnostic that lets you skip what you already know. This page does the same job for English, for the whole track at once. It does **not** replace any stage: it tells you which stages to test out of and where to begin.

**How to take it**
- Cold: no notes, no spell checker, no grammar checker, no AI. Paper is fine.
- One part per session is fine. Do the parts in order, and stop at the first part you fail. Everything after that point starts as normal.
- Mark your answers yourself from the collapsible answer blocks. Be strict: a misspelling is wrong even if you "knew it".
- Log the result in your journal (`FND-EN-PLACEMENT`, with your score for each part).

**How to use the score** (the same rule as the math diagnostics)

| Part | Stages it covers | Pass mark | If you pass |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| A | E01–E03 (spelling, word parts, confusables) | 23/25 | Do the **self-checks** of E01, E02 and E03 cold. Each one you pass (its own target) is done: tick it in [Start Here](<../../00 - Start Here.md>) and note "placed out". |
| B | E04 (parts of speech) | 9/10 | Do the E04 self-check cold; if you pass it, E04 is done. |
| C | E05 (sentences and verbs) | 9/10 | Do the E05 self-check cold; if you pass it, E05 is done. |
| D | E06 (punctuation) | 9/10 fixes | Do the E06 self-check cold; if you pass it, E06 is done. |
| E | E07 (joining ideas) | 5/6 | Do the E07 self-check cold; if you pass it, E07 is done. |
| F | E08 (paragraphs and clarity) | all 5 checklist items | Do the E08 self-check cold; if you pass it, E08 is done. |
| G | E09–E10 (technical writing and argument) | all checklist items | Do the E09 and E10 self-checks cold; each one you pass is done. |

**Start the track** at the first stage you did not place out of. Two rules:
- **Projects still count.** A project attached to a skipped stage (for example the [Spelling Engine](projects/spelling-engine/spec.md) or the [First Design Doc](projects/first-design-doc/spec.md)) is still on the checklist, because later modules build on it. Do it alongside your current stage.
- **The habits stay.** Copywork [C] and your spelling error log run in every English session whatever stage you are in. Your E01 baseline (Part 1 of E01) is still worth recording: it is the "before" picture for your progress graph.

## Part A — Spelling and confusable words (E01–E03)

**A1. Dictation (20 words).** Record yourself reading these aloud, one per line, then in a later session play the recording and write the words without looking at this page.

> separate · definitely · necessary · receive · believe · occurred · beginning · argument · truly · library · government · environment · business · address · across · accommodate · writing · written · studying · noticeable

**A2. Pick the right word (5 items).**
1. ___ going to deploy ___ code over ___. (they're / their / there)
2. The script checks ___ own config before ___ too late. (its / it's)
3. If ___ ready, open ___ terminal. (your / you're)
4. The new cache had a big ___ on speed. (affect / effect)
5. This version is faster ___ the old one. (then / than)

<details>
<summary>Answers (Part A)</summary>

A1: the 20 words exactly as printed above. One point per word.

A2 (one point per item, all blanks right):
1. **They're** going to deploy **their** code over **there**.
2. The script checks **its** own config before **it's** too late.
3. If **you're** ready, open **your** terminal.
4. **effect** (a noun: "a big effect"; *affect* is usually the verb).
5. **than** (comparison; *then* is about order).

Score out of 25.
</details>

## Part B — Parts of speech (E04)

Name the part of speech of each numbered word:

> The **old**(1) **laptop**(2) **runs**(3) **slowly**(4), **but**(5) **it**(6) still works **on**(7) **my**(8) desk. **Wow**(9), it **boots**(10)!

<details>
<summary>Answers (Part B)</summary>

1 adjective · 2 noun · 3 verb · 4 adverb · 5 conjunction · 6 pronoun · 7 preposition · 8 possessive (accept "pronoun", "possessive pronoun" or "determiner") · 9 interjection · 10 verb. Score out of 10.
</details>

## Part C — Sentences and verbs (E05)

1–3. Complete sentence or fragment? (a) *Because the build failed.* (b) *Run the tests.* (c) *The server that I set up yesterday.*
4–5. Choose the verb: (a) *The list of files (is / are) long.* (b) *Each of the tests (pass / passes).*
6–9. Write the simple past and the past participle: *write · begin · choose · run*.
10. Rewrite in the active voice: *The bug was found by Sam.*

<details>
<summary>Answers (Part C)</summary>

1 fragment (a dependent clause on its own) · 2 complete (an imperative; the subject "you" is understood) · 3 fragment (no main verb for "the server") · 4 **is** (the subject is *list*) · 5 **passes** (*each* is singular) · 6 wrote, written · 7 began, begun · 8 chose, chosen · 9 ran, run · 10 *Sam found the bug.* Score out of 10 (items 6–9 need both forms).
</details>

## Part D — Punctuation (E06)

Rewrite with correct capitals and punctuation. There are at least 10 fixes.

> my laptop which i bought used has three ports usb hdmi and ethernet its fast enough for python but the fan is loud

<details>
<summary>Answers (Part D)</summary>

*My laptop, which I bought used, has three ports: USB, HDMI, and Ethernet. It's fast enough for Python, but the fan is loud.*

Fixes: capital *My* · comma before *which* · *I* · comma after *used* · colon after *ports* · *USB* · *HDMI* plus its list comma · comma before *and* (serial comma) · *Ethernet* · full stop after *Ethernet* · *It's* (with apostrophe, capitalised) · *Python* · comma before *but*. Count one point per fix you made correctly, up to 10.
</details>

## Part E — Joining ideas (E07)

1–2. Fix each run-on two different ways: *The test failed I fixed the bug.*
3. Join with *because*: *The page loads slowly. The images are huge.*
4. Join with *although*: *The code works. It is hard to read.*
5. Make the list parallel: *I like coding, to read, and swimming.*
6. Fragment or not? *Which is why the script stops.*

<details>
<summary>Answers (Part E)</summary>

1–2 any two of: *The test failed, so I fixed the bug.* · *The test failed; I fixed the bug.* · *The test failed. I fixed the bug.* · *When the test failed, I fixed the bug.* · 3 *The page loads slowly because the images are huge.* · 4 *Although the code works, it is hard to read.* (or *The code works, although it is hard to read.*) · 5 *I like coding, reading, and swimming.* · 6 fragment (a relative clause on its own). Score out of 6.
</details>

## Part F — A paragraph (E08)

Write one paragraph (about 120 words) explaining how to do one everyday task you know well, for someone who has never done it. Then check it against this list:

1. The first sentence says what the whole paragraph is about (a topic sentence).
2. Every other sentence supports that one idea.
3. Each sentence starts with something the reader already knows and ends with the new information (old before new).
4. No wordy phrases (*in order to*, *due to the fact that*, *at this point in time*).
5. You read it aloud and nothing made you stumble.

**Pass:** all five are true. Ask a friend to check item 5 if you can.

## Part G — Technical writing and argument (E09–E10)

1. **Instructions:** write numbered instructions for installing and running one program you use. Each step is one action, starts with a verb, and says what the reader should see afterwards.
2. **Argument:** in about 200 words, argue for one of two tools you have used (two editors, two browsers, two languages). State your claim first, give two reasons with evidence, and name one real trade-off of your choice.

**Pass:** someone else follows your instructions without asking you a question, **and** your argument has a claim first, two reasons with evidence, and a trade-off.
