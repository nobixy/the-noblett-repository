# Appendix E — Failure Modes, In Order of Frequency
*Review this note whenever motivation drops or progress feels stalled.*

1. **Course collecting:** Twelve things enrolled, none finished.  
   *Fix:* Rule 3 — Never start a new block until the current one is done.

2. **Watching lectures instead of doing problems:** The most seductive failure — P1 explains why it feels like learning.  
   *Fix:* Rule 1 — No lecture without its problem set the same week.

3. **Skipping math, hitting a wall in Year 3, quitting.**  
   *Fix:* Rule 4 — Math and writing are daily habits and are never paused.

4. **Skipping writing, then stalling at the design-doc stage at work.**  
   *Fix:* Habit 1 — Write 500 words a day.

5. **Tutorial-difficulty projects.**  
   *Fix:* Every build must contain a part where you're stuck for a day.

6. **No feedback loop.**  
   *Fix:* Publish, contribute to open source, find a study partner, apply to Recurse Center.

7. **LeetCode as curriculum:** It teaches pattern-matching, not design.  
   *Fix:* Six-week sprint before job hunting, not before.

8. **Comparing to other people's highlight reels.**  
   *Fix:* Compare to your own daily log from six months ago.

9. **Building on sand:** If you can't explain cache lines or process-vs-thread cold, you're not ready for distributed systems.  
   *Fix:* The "Done when" lines, honestly applied.

10. **A 50-hour week you can't sustain.**  
    *Fix:* Pick the honest pace (e.g. 20 hrs/wk) and hold it for years.

11. **Skipping P1 and P2 because they don't feel like "real" CS.**  
    *Fix:* They're the two blocks that determine whether the other 33 get done.
