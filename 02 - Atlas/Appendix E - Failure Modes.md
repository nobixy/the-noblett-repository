# Appendix E — Failure Modes, In Order of Frequency
*Review this note whenever motivation drops or progress feels stalled.*

1. **Course collecting:** Twelve things enrolled, none finished.  
   *Fix:* Rule 2 in [[00 - Start Here|Start Here]] — one build project at a time.

2. **Watching lectures instead of doing problems:** The most seductive failure — [[LM08 - Chunking and the Illusion of Competence|LM08]] explains why it feels like learning.  
   *Fix:* The V protocol in [study-protocols](<../study-protocols.md>) — no video without its practice in the same session; do more than you watch.

3. **Skipping math, hitting a wall in a later module, quitting.**  
   *Fix:* Math and English sessions rotate with build sessions in [the session loop](<../study-protocols.md#the-session-loop>) and are never dropped.

4. **Skipping writing, then stalling at the design-doc stage at work.**  
   *Fix:* Copywork and the fun write in every English session; English E08–E10.

5. **Tutorial-difficulty projects.**  
   *Fix:* Every build must contain a part where you get properly stuck (then use the stuck rule).

6. **No feedback loop.**  
   *Fix:* Publish, contribute to open source, find a study partner, apply to Recurse Center.

7. **LeetCode as curriculum:** It teaches pattern-matching, not design.  
   *Fix:* Use problem sites as a supplement — a few per section, mixed across topics — never as the curriculum ([Module 05 resources](<../05-data-structures-and-algorithms/resources.md>)).

8. **Comparing to other people's highlight reels.**  
   *Fix:* Compare to your own session log from many sections ago.

9. **Building on sand:** If you can't explain cache lines or process-vs-thread cold, you're not ready for distributed systems.  
   *Fix:* The "Done when" lines, honestly applied.

10. **A pace you can't sustain.**  
    *Fix:* Regular ordinary sessions beat binges. When a session goes wrong, use the minimum session (Rule 3); after a gap, restart with one normal session (Rule 6).

11. **Skipping the foundations (English and math) because they don't feel like "real" CS.**  
    *Fix:* They determine whether the modules get done. Use the placement checks to skip what you already know, not to skip the tracks.
