# LM11 — Focused and Diffuse Thinking
*Deep dive + project ([[DR-009 - Learning Method Deep Dives|DR-009]]). Hub: [[LM00 - Learning Methods Hub|Learning Methods]] · Method in practice: [[how-i-study]] §1*

> [!NOTE] Evidence: **Useful metaphor; incubation evidence moderate**

## In plain words
Work hard on a problem with full attention (focused mode). When you're truly stuck, step away to something light, like a walk or chores (diffuse mode), and come back. Often the answer turns up.

## The evidence, honestly
"Focused vs diffuse" is Barbara Oakley's teaching metaphor, not a precise brain model.
The nearest tested idea is incubation. Sio & Ormerod (2009), a meta-analysis, found a real but modest benefit from setting a problem aside. It was larger after a long first attempt, and light activity during the break helped more than demanding tasks.
So: only take the break after a real attempt.

## Using it in EECS
- Use it for stuck bugs, proofs and design questions: write down exactly where you're stuck, walk, then come back and reread that note first.

## Common mistakes
- Using "diffuse mode" as permission to quit after one attempt.
- Taking the break on something demanding, like social media or another hard problem.

## 🔨 Project: Stuck-Log experiment
Across two sections, whenever you're still stuck after three honest attempts (a Bandit level, a proof, a bug), log it. Alternate: on odd-numbered episodes keep pushing; on even-numbered ones take a walk first. Record whether you solved it soon after going back.
- **Done when:** at least 8 stuck episodes are logged, with a 3-sentence conclusion that admits how small the sample is.
- **Level:** No code (optional chart).
- **Fits with:** Protocol **D** (diffuse break) and the stuck rule: three honest attempts, then a stuck note and a break.

## Sources
- Oakley, *A Mind for Numbers* (2014), ch. 2.
- Sio & Ormerod (2009), "Does incubation enhance problem solving? A meta-analytic review", *Psychological Bulletin* 135(1), 94–120.
