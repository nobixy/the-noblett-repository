# Learning Styles: Why There Isn't One for You
*Part of the [[LM00 - Learning Methods Hub|Learning Methods]] deep dives ([[DR-009 - Learning Method Deep Dives|DR-009]]).*

You asked for a project for "each learning style". In the research sense, "learning styles" means the idea that each person is a visual, auditory, reading/writing or kinesthetic learner (e.g. VARK), and learns best when teaching matches that type. **That idea is not supported.**
- **Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer & Bjork (2008):** to support it, studies would need a "crossover": visual learners do better with visual teaching *and* auditory learners do better with auditory teaching. They found almost no studies designed to show this, and the well-designed ones contradicted it.
- **What is true:**
  - People have preferences.
  - The *content* decides the best format: circuits need diagrams, proofs need symbols.
  - Everyone gains from words plus pictures ([[LM04 - Dual Coding|dual coding]]).
  - Everyone gains from techniques like [[LM02 - Retrieval Practice|retrieval]] and [[LM09 - Spacing and Spaced Repetition|spacing]].
- **What you really meant:** "learning methods and frameworks". Those have deep dives and projects in the [[LM00 - Learning Methods Hub|hub]].
- **Your "I learn best by doing projects"** is still a good rule. It works because projects force retrieval, self-explanation and feedback, not because you are a "kinesthetic type".

**Source:** Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer & Bjork (2008), "Learning styles: concepts and evidence", *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* 9(3), 105–119.
